# BETWEEN TWO SUNS — Ingredient Education & Conversion Copy Brief for Opus

## Core principle
Consumers should understand not only WHAT is in each product, but WHY it is there, what role it plays in the formula, and why the combination makes the product better.

The tone must be:
- cool
- modern
- intelligent
- concise
- consumer-friendly
- never textbook-clinical
- never pseudo-scientific
- never fear-based ingredient marketing
- never overclaim

The content hierarchy should translate formulation science into useful buying language:
1. WHY IT'S HERE
2. WHAT IT DOES
3. WHY IT MATTERS IN THIS PRODUCT
4. HOW THE FORMULA WORKS AS A SYSTEM

Avoid generic ingredient dictionary cards that could sit on any skincare website.

## Consumer-facing style examples
Prefer:
- "Clean, not squeaky."
- "Oil off. Comfort stays."
- "A brightening serum that doesn't forget hydration."
- "Barrier support without the heavy finish."
- "SPF that earns its place in your morning."

Avoid:
- "This potent botanical antioxidant synergistically combats oxidative stress..."
- "Toxin-free / chemical-free / clean beauty"
- demonizing sulfates, silicones, preservatives, alcohols, or synthetic ingredients as categories
- claims that cannot be substantiated by the final formula/tests

## DAILY DEFENSE SUNSCREEN SPF 50
Do not build ingredient education from generic sunscreen-filter knowledge until the final production INCI/filter system and SPF/UVA testing are supplied.

Consumer education should focus on:
- verified SPF 50
- verified UVA / broad-spectrum status
- filter system only after exact INCI is known
- finish
- white cast
- pilling
- makeup compatibility
- reapplication
- amount needed
- skin-tone testing, if performed

Potential tonal direction:
"Protection you'll actually want to wear."
The most converting SPF education is not a chemistry lecture; it answers: Will it cast? pill? feel greasy? work under makeup? and how much should I use?

## Ingredient education UI
Use 3 layers:

### Layer 1 — 3-second scan
2–4 short reasons to care.
Examples:
- SULFATE-FREE CLEANSING
- 4% GLYCERIN
- 5% NIACINAMIDE
- 3% TRANEXAMIC ACID
- CERAMIDE SUPPORT
Only show percentages that are confirmed and legally usable.

### Layer 2 — "Why it's here"
Tap/expand:
Ingredient name
→ what it does in plain language
→ why it matters in THIS formula

Example:
**Glycerin — 4%**
"A humectant that helps hold onto water. In a cleanser, that matters because clean skin shouldn't have to feel stripped."

### Layer 3 — "Formula logic"
Explain combinations, not isolated hero ingredients.

Example:
**Why three cleansers?**
"Different cleansing agents behave differently on skin. We use a blend so the formula can lift oil and buildup effectively without leaning on one aggressive surfactant."

Example:
**Why Niacinamide + Tranexamic Acid?**
"They work on uneven-looking tone through different pathways, while the hydrating base keeps daily use comfortable."

## Content rule
Never make every ingredient a hero.
The story is:
- 2–4 hero ingredients / technologies
- 1 formula-system explanation
- full INCI available for transparency
- deep ingredient glossary accessible but not forced into the primary conversion path

## Evidence / claim discipline
Before publishing:
- verify final production INCI
- verify final concentrations
- verify pH where relevant
- verify SPF/UVA-PF / broad-spectrum data
- verify clinical / consumer / instrumental claims
- verify "non-comedogenic," "dermatologist tested," "sensitive skin," "no white cast," etc. only if actually tested
- do not infer an efficacy claim solely from ingredient presence
- rinse-off ingredients receive more conservative benefit language than leave-on products

## CRO role of ingredient education
Ingredient content must reduce purchase anxiety and make the value proposition clearer:
- WHY this formula is different
- WHY the ingredient is there
- WHY it fits the skin concern
- WHY the whole formula makes sense
It should increase confidence, not increase cognitive load.
