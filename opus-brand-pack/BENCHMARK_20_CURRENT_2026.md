## Cross-benchmark lessons for BTS
- Strong brands do not choose between art direction and commerce; product desirability and purchase action coexist.
- A tiny four-SKU catalog should feel intentional, not empty. The routine can be a primary purchasable object.
- Sensory media matters most when it answers a buying question: texture, finish, scale, application.
- Product cards need a real role/benefit, price and immediate action—not decorative tiles.
- PDPs should progressively answer what/why/feel/how/fit/trust, while keeping ATC persistent on mobile.
- Color systems work when they encode navigation/state/step, not when they are merely pastel card backgrounds.
- Editorial storytelling works best as a distinct layer; it must not block shopping.
- Bundles should show contents, individual value, set value and why items belong together.
- Search/concern discovery should scale for 20+ SKUs, but four launch SKUs do not justify filter theatre.
- Trust is strongest when specific: real reviews, real claims, delivery/COD/payment clarity, full INCI, clear support.
- Mobile interaction must be native: swipe, thumb-safe controls, sticky purchase action after the primary CTA leaves view.
- Localization is composition, not translation. Arabic requires its own typography, line breaks, bidi handling and motion logic.
- Performance is a design constraint: static LCP hero, lazy below-fold media, no mandatory intro/WebGL.
- Ownable interaction should be direct manipulation, not passive scroll spectacle.
- BTS should borrow no brand's visual shorthand. Its ownable primitives remain )(, exact wordmark, four pack colors, texture/swatch, and AM→PM routine logic.
